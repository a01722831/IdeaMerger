# Report: startup discovery for a solo, AI-native founder

Prepared 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-01. Everything here is backed by files in this repo; every pain claim traces to a signal with a URL, a date and an exact quote in [research/SIGNALS.md](research/SIGNALS.md).

## 1. Executive summary
**The short version.** After 571 sourced pain signals, 216 seeds and 41 competition-checked idea files, no idea scored above 64 out of 100, and that is the honest result. In late 2026 the big assistants and vertical incumbents absorbed most generic AI use cases, and 24 of the 41 developed ideas died as crowded, almost all in small-business back office. What survived are long-lived case files where a family or tiny operator faces a slow institution and the incumbent is a human service priced in thousands of dollars. The top four (Medicaid Ready, Contents Claim Builder, Grant Ledger, Benefits Portal Pilot) are worth a week of testing each; the rest are options. Every idea's weakest point is proven willingness to pay, so every first test below is a payment test.

The top 10 (ranks 9 to 11 tie at 52.6; Do It With Me and Chat-to-Ledger were picked over Authority Guard for diversity, because Authority Guard shares Haul Paid's buyer and scores 2 of 5 on AI leverage):

**1. [Medicaid Ready](ideas/006-medicaid-ready.md) (63.2, weak direct competition, web app).** For families placing a parent in nursing or memory care. They upload up to five years of bank statements; it builds a month-by-month table, flags look-back transfers next to the state's published rule text, drafts factual explanation letters from the family's own answers, fills the state application and tracks agency requests. Evidence: a facility demanding statements two days before Christmas (LIFE-17), a $15K lawyer versus $6K service quote (LIFE-18), adult children buried in a parent's paperwork (LIFE-13, LIFE-16). About 1.24M people live in certified nursing facilities and Medicaid is the main payer for 63% of them (KFF, in the file). Competitors are human services and MedicaidSoft, which sells to elder-law attorneys, not families. Price $199 to $349 once, about $4 of model cost per case. Biggest risk: the flagged-transfer report sits near unauthorized practice of law, and facility-linked vendors control referrals. First test: a $40 landing page with hand-built previews and a $49 refundable deposit (pass: 6+ real submissions and 2+ deposits from 60+ visitors), plus a written opinion from an elder-law attorney in the launch state.

**2. [Contents Claim Builder](ideas/039-contents-claim-builder.md) (61.1, weak direct, web app).** For households after a fire, flood or wildfire, and for the public adjusters who represent them. It rebuilds the item-by-item contents list insurers demand (3,000+ items for one household, GAP-02) from old phone photos, videos, email order history and card exports, priced at replacement cost in the insurer's format. This has the strongest pain evidence in the project: a full spreadsheet was what finally forced a payout (GAP-01), and households pay adjusters 12.5% to 15% (GAP-04, GAP-05). Weaknesses: free and cheap inventory apps (Bevel, Loti, ProofList), InventoryQuant (YC W26) on the professional side, and paid claim preparation needs an adjuster license in many states (Florida exempts inventorying), so the household tier is reconstruction only. Price $249 per household or $149 a month per adjuster. First test: a concierge run for 5 survivors and 2 adjusters; pass if 3 households and 1 adjuster pay or commit and order history adds 30%+ evidenced items.

**3. [Grant Ledger](ideas/005-grant-ledger.md) (60.0, weak direct, web app on QuickBooks or Xero).** For small nonprofits. It reads each grant agreement (allowable costs, budget lines, periods) and proposes an allocation for every expense with the clause cited, for the finance lead to approve, then drafts funder reports, budget-versus-actuals and board packs. Evidence: two days lost reconstructing which grant paid for what (PRO-208), funder reports built by hand (PRO-211, PRO-212), $10K to $15K paid to consultants for data work (PRO-209). The IRS master file counts 169,515 public charities with $250K to $5M of income. Competition is thin, but Intuit is building AI for nonprofits. Price $149 a month, margin above 90%. Biggest risks: salary allocation (v1 must include time-and-effort splits) and whether auditors accept the trail. First test: free manual allocations for 5 organizations; pass if 38 of 40 spot-checked lines agree and 2+ pay a $99 deposit.

**4. [Benefits Portal Pilot](ideas/027-benefits-portal-pilot.md) (58.9, adjacent competition only, browser extension).** For benefits navigators at health centers, hospitals and legal aid. Medicaid work requirements start on 2027-01-01: expansion enrollees must document 80 hours a month (FORCE-11), and enrollees fear losing coverage (FORCE-13 to FORCE-15). A side panel walks the navigator through each state portal page by page, pre-fills from client documents with approval, and builds work-requirement proof packets. It is the clearest HeyClicky-style idea here, with a hard regulatory date. Weaknesses: no navigator employer has been shown paying, grant-funded open-source form fillers (Nava/Amplifi, Code for America with Anthropic) do similar work free, and HIPAA plus dozens of portals is heavy for one person. Price $99 a month per seat. First test: a $33 payer smoke test (40 tailored emails to four payer types); pass if 2+ calls name a budget line and 1+ signs a paid pilot letter at $60+ a seat.

**5. [Parent Mail Desk](ideas/007-parent-mail-desk.md) (56.8, weak direct, SMS plus web).** For adult children of aging parents. The parent texts a photo of any letter; AI explains it in plain language, flags scams and deadlines, and gives the child a one-tap action (pay, call script, reply, file) in a shared family case log. Evidence: parents photograph every letter and ask one child to explain it (LIFE-14); a daughter takes over a widowed mother's bills, pension and logins (LIFE-13). Weaknesses: explaining a letter is free in every assistant, Antigrift already sells a text-a-photo scam check at $9 to $19 a month, no one has shown paying, and the product as scoped stops before the painful part (calling and proving authority). Price $14.99 a month per family. First test: a $40 concierge week with 8 families; pass if 5 parents text 4+ photos unprompted and 3 children pay.

**6. [Haul Paid](ideas/001-haul-paid.md) (55.8, weak direct, mobile-friendly web app).** For owner-operators and fleets of 1 to 10 trucks. They forward rate confirmations, BOLs, lumper receipts and ELD exports; it builds a per-load claim for detention, layover and short-pays and runs the escalation ladder in the carrier's own name, up to a surety-bond claim. Evidence: brokers refusing detention that rate cons promise (PRO-029), short-pays of $100 to $375 on $1,000 loads (PRO-030), a $650 detention claim rejected over a missing BOL time (GAP-20). FMCSA data shows roughly 200K to 334K small for-hire carriers. Weaknesses: Hey Bubba already files detention claims from ELD data, and a contingency fee may need a collection-agency license in many states, so v1 is a $29-a-month tool. First test: a free 7-day manual claim review; pass if 8+ real loads arrive from 5+ carriers, 90%+ of extracted terms are right and 3+ carriers leave a $10 deposit.

**7. [Paper Trail](ideas/008-paper-trail.md) (53.7, weak direct, web app plus mail).** For households facing IRS, SSA or state notices. Photograph a notice; get its meaning, a drafted reply, tracked certified mail and deadline reminders, with escalation through the Taxpayer Advocate Service or a congressional casework request when stuck. Evidence is broad: refunds stuck for months, cryptic Social Security suspensions, unreachable phone lines (LIFE-27, LIFE-29, REV-47). Weaknesses: OweRight launched a near-identical app on 2026-10-23, the IRS's free upload tool confirms receipt, and composing replies for a fee raises a Circular 230 question. Price $29 for a draft, $59 to send and track. First test: an honest pre-order page; pass if 5+ of 150 visitors leave a $9 deposit and half choose $59. The best re-scope is Social Security and state-agency notices.

**8. [Viva Voce](ideas/018-viva-voce.md) (53.7, weak direct, voice web app).** For instructors facing AI-written work. After a student submits, a 5 to 8 minute realtime voice conversation grounded in that submission checks they understand it; the instructor gets a transcript, rubric scores and flagged inconsistencies. It answers a real classroom crisis (AI2-47, AI2-48, AI2-52) and is affordable only because realtime voice now costs about $0.05 a minute. Weaknesses: instructors lack budgets, student-data rules put the decision with institutions, FeedbackFruits already sells LMS-integrated oral assessment to 200+ institutions, and the method is easy to copy. Price $1.50 per viva or a department license. First test: run it with 6 volunteer instructors for one assignment; pass if 4 say it saved time and one department names a budget for a pilot.

**9. [Do It With Me](ideas/036-do-it-with-me.md) (52.6, weak direct, voice guide).** For older adults and the adult children who support them. The child sets up routines (pay the electric bill, book a portal appointment); the parent asks for help and an on-screen voice guide walks them through with pointing, refuses scam-like steps and calls the child when stuck. It is the HeyClicky pattern applied where trust matters most, with real demand (HN-21 would "pay good money"; booking in MyChart needs a grandchild, UND-16). Weaknesses: Gemini Live, Copilot Vision and iOS 27 Siri already guide on screen for free, Android and iOS restrict accessibility services and screen capture in banking apps, and no one has paid. First test: a platform baseline (do the free assistants already stop scams and hand off to family?), then a Wizard-of-Oz trial with 10 families at $15 a month.

**10. [Chat-to-Ledger](ideas/033-chat-to-ledger.md) (52.6, weak direct, chat-based; no deep dive).** For WhatsApp-first micro-sellers in Brazil, Mexico and India. Owners forward order chats, voice notes and payment receipts; AI keeps orders, customers and payment promises in a ledger outside WhatsApp, sends reminders and matches Pix, UPI or SPEI transfers. Evidence: businesses run on WhatsApp plus a paper notebook (UND-21), chase late payers by hand (UND-22), lost catalog order building (UND-24). Weaknesses: owners resist paying (a reply-bot builder reached zero sales from 384 businesses, UND-20), free ledger apps (Khatabook, OkCredit) and Meta's own Business Agent sit in the channel, and WhatsApp bans make it fragile. Price $6 a month. First test: a 3-week concierge with 15 sellers; pass if 85% of orders are captured correctly and 10 of 15 keep forwarding.

## 3. Method
**Pipeline.** Landscape scan, then ten pain-mining tracks, then 216 seeds from nine lenses plus wild ideas, screened against red zones and kill criteria, then 41 full idea files with a competition check of at least four differently worded searches each, then calibration, deep dives and red team on the top 12. Research subagents did the mining, drafting and deep dives; the main researcher did ideation, screening, review, kills, calibration and synthesis.

**Sources and counts.**
| Step | Output |
|---|---|
| Landscape | 95 recent AI products (Product Hunt via mirrors, YC W25 to F26 batch counts, BetaList, Uneed, Microlaunch, Futurepedia); model price tables from official pages read 2026-10-31; 20 investor theses; YC's Winter 2027 Requests for Startups; 26 dead or absorbed AI products |
| Pain mining | 586 raw signals in 10 tracks (Hacker News 38, founder communities 39, three professions batches 116, incumbent reviews 51, service-to-software 63, life admin 55, second-order AI 60, forcing functions 56, underserved users 51, gap-fill 46, plus 11 from the landscape), consolidated to **571 distinct signals, 130 strong**, in 62 themes |
| Ideation | **216 seeds**: Pain-first 31, Newly possible 24, Remix 22, Transplant 16, Service-to-software 25, Second-order AI 23, Interaction shift 16, Forcing function 20, Underserved users 27, Wild 12 |
| Screening | 109 killed at screening (reason recorded), 63 merged into stronger seeds, 41 developed, 3 queued for a second wave (not run) |
| Development | **41 full idea files**; **25 killed after their competition check or red team** (all CROWDED), **16 live** |
| Top 12 | Deep dives and red-team critiques with responses and score changes |

**Evidence rules.** Every pain claim has a URL, a date when visible and an exact quote of two sentences or fewer; quotes were machine-checked against the fetched text in every track. Estimates are tagged [ESTIMATE] with the math, assumptions [ASSUMPTION], unverifiable claims [UNVERIFIED].

**What was blocked.** Reddit refuses direct reads from this environment; exact Reddit text came from the public Arctic Shift archive (newest posts per subreddit, so older threads are underrepresented). Product Hunt, G2, Trustpilot, Upwork, Fiverr, Indie Hackers, Peerlist and There's An AI For That returned 403; Product Hunt was read through mirrors (hunted.space, aitoolly), Upwork and Fiverr through public category pages, press and Freelancer.com listings. Paywalled news (FT, Bloomberg, 404 Media) was used for headlines only. Full register: [research/SOURCES.md](research/SOURCES.md).

## 4. Landscape
Full synthesis: [research/LANDSCAPE.md](research/LANDSCAPE.md).
- **The big assistants absorbed the horizontal layer in 2026.** ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork and Claude Duet, Gemini tasks, Copilot Vision and Autopilot browse, act, remember, make documents, see the screen and run proactively. OpenAI retired its own Atlas browser and Sora. Generic agents, screen overlays, memory layers and document generators are red zones.
- **Incumbents shipped their own AI.** Intuit's Accounting Agent, Jobber's voice quotes, ServiceTitan's Atlas, Vagaro's Fill My Books, AppFolio's Realm-X, Stripe Smart Disputes and Greenhouse Voice AI each erased a startup wedge this project tested (section 5).
- **Small models are cheap and voice is a commodity.** Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna cost $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens, so a typical 3,000-in, 500-out call costs about $0.00055; realtime voice is about $0.05 a minute. Model cost is rarely the constraint for the ideas below; trust, liability and distribution are.
- **Investor consensus is crowded.** "Sell outcomes, not tools" in insurance, accounting, healthcare revenue cycle and legal is the most crowded thesis and mostly enterprise-sold. YC's newest requests moved to bottlenecks: credentials, government paperwork, the aging population, proving you are human, compliance.
- **HeyClicky set the interaction bar and showed the risk.** AI by the cursor that sees the screen on a hotkey and talks you through tasks won distribution, was cloned six times and absorbed by labs within months. The defensible version ties that interaction to a narrow job, domain data or a system the labs ignore.
- **Rules changed in 2025 and 2026.** The end of de minimis, the IEEPA tariff refunds, CPSC eFiling, EU packaging and GPSR rules, the European Accessibility Act, Medicaid work requirements from 2027 and FMCSA's Motus migration all create document-heavy work. Six expected forcing functions were vacated, stayed or delayed (FinCEN real-estate rule, CMMC Phase II, EU AI Act high-risk dates, Colorado AI Act, the H-1B fee, a student-visa rule), so deadlines need checking before building on them.

## 5. Opportunity themes
**What the competition checks taught.** 25 of 41 developed ideas died as CROWDED (24 at review, one after red team), and the pattern is clear. Generic small-business back office is saturated in late 2026: bookkeeping catch-up and review, receipt capture, chargebacks, booking-gap filling, voice estimates for trades, wholesale order entry, maintenance dispatch, change orders, restaurant invoices, COI issuance, applicant screening, AI governance for MSPs, accessibility fixes and remediation, product-compliance documents, tariff refunds and payment-fraud checks all have funded entrants or incumbents' own AI features. Consumer medical-bill fighting and AI quote checking are crowded too.

**Where whitespace survived.** The live ideas share three traits: (1) a long-lived case file rather than a one-shot answer, (2) a counterparty institution (a Medicaid agency, an insurer, a broker, a court, a funder, an agency portal) that is slow or adversarial, and (3) an incumbent that is a human service priced in thousands of dollars or a percentage, not software. The main cost in these markets is trust, liability lines and distribution, not model spend.

## 7. Wildcards
Unconventional seeds worth a second look even though they were not advanced (full list in [ideas/POOL.md](ideas/POOL.md)):
- **Casework Copilot (W01):** prepares a congressional casework request when the IRS, SSA, VA or USCIS stalls; folded into Paper Trail as an escalation channel, and possibly the most distinctive feature that idea has.
- **Regulatory Twin (W02):** a live model of every rule that applies to one micro-business; too broad for a solo MVP, but the right long-term shape for compliance products.
- **Viva Voce (018)** is the live idea closest to a wildcard: oral defenses of student work at class scale, only affordable since realtime voice became a commodity.
- **Wave 2, not run (S226 to S228):** long-term-care insurance claims for families, court accountings for family guardians and special-needs trustees, and workers' comp premium-audit defense for small contractors. They sit squarely in the whitespace pattern above and are the first thing to test in an EXTEND pass.

## 9. Limitations and blind spots
- **Scores are judgment, not measurement.** The rubric is applied consistently and recalibrated side by side, but a 60 versus a 58 is not a meaningful difference. Confidence is MED or LOW for every live idea; none has a paying customer yet.
- **Willingness to pay is the weakest evidence everywhere.** Pain signals are strong; explicit payment signals are rare. Every top idea's first test is a payment test for that reason.
- **Coverage gaps.** Reddit evidence skews to the newest posts per subreddit (archive limits). Product Hunt, G2, Upwork and Fiverr were read indirectly. Professions with little online voice (funeral homes, churches, small farms, dental labs) produced no usable evidence, which says more about where people post than about their pain. The research is US-heavy, with some EU, Latin American and Indian signals.
- **Competition checks age fast.** Several competitors launched weeks before this research (OweRight on 2026-10-23, Greenhouse Voice AI in September 2026, many AI apps in 2026). Re-run the searches in each idea's section 10 before building.
- **Legal lines are not legal advice.** Several ideas sit near unauthorized practice of law, public adjusting, debt collection, CMS marketing rules or Circular 230. The files flag each line and how the product stays clear of it; a short consult with a practitioner in the launch state is part of each validation plan.
- **Platform risk is high across the board.** Frontier labs and vertical incumbents shipped features in 2026 that killed many seeds; the same can happen to live ideas. Each file has a platform-risk paragraph.
- **Second wave not run.** Three seeds in the strongest whitespace pattern (S226 to S228) were queued but not developed because of usage limits.

## 10. Suggested decision process
Each top idea has a one-week test under $50 with a pass/fail threshold set in advance (in its `## Deep dive`, "One-week validation test"). Run them as payment tests, not interest tests.

**Week 1 (in parallel, about $120 total):**
1. **Medicaid Ready:** the $40 landing page with hand-built previews and a $49 refundable deposit. In the same week, book one hour with an elder-law attorney in the launch state to confirm the counts-only preview and family-voice letters are not practice of law (outside the $50 budget).
2. **Grant Ledger:** free manual allocations for 5 nonprofits from their agreement PDF and a 3-month export (pass: 38 of 40 lines agree, 2+ pay a $99 deposit).
3. **Contents Claim Builder:** the licensing map for 10 states plus 3 volunteer households (pass: 4 of 10 states clear, order history adds 30%+ evidenced items in 2 of 3 households).

**Week 2:** the payer smoke test for **Benefits Portal Pilot** (40 tailored emails to health centers, hospitals, legal aid and plans). It has the biggest upside and the least payer evidence, so it is worth one week only if a budget holder answers. If you prefer a non-family market, swap in **Haul Paid**'s manual claim review.

**Decision rules:**
- Build the first idea that passes its threshold and its legal check. Before writing code, re-run its section 10 searches; competition moved monthly during this research.
