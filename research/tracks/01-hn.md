## Method
Source: Hacker News only, via the public Algolia HN API (exact comment text, dates, permalinks). No WebSearch or WebFetch was used. Read-only throughout. Window: comments and posts from 2025-05-01 onward (earliest signal 2025-05-16, latest 2026-10-28).

Tools. The first 14 calls used `scripts/hn_search.py` directly. Because generic phrase queries returned mostly noise, I then used small scratch wrappers that import `get()` and `strip_html()` from `scripts/hn_search.py` (same endpoint, same text cleaning): a single-query lister with optional-word support, a bulk fetcher (up to 1000 hits per query, cached locally and filtered offline with regexes for first-person pain language), and a thread reader (`/items/<id>`, whole tree including nested replies). Every quote below was checked by script as an exact substring of the cleaned API text (whitespace-normalized). Quotes are trimmed with [...] only.

Selection rules. One signal per thread or story (the best comment; distinct commenters); other comments from the same thread are cited only in the observations. Skipped pure developer-tool and coding-assistant wishes. Excluded comments flagged `[dead]` and obvious product pitches with no personal pain. Where the poster is the vendor of a product or is validating an idea, the "who" cell says so and strength is capped at medium. Result: 38 signals (13 strong, 17 medium, 8 weak).

## Patterns and observations
Interpretation is labeled as such. Extra comment ids cited here were read in the same API output but are not counted as separate signals (one signal per thread).

- Tax and bookkeeping is the largest cluster (HN-01 to HN-06). People already use general chatbots both to replace a paid preparer and to audit one. Same-thread data points: $3-4k in preparation fees (50277832), a refund "nearly $2k lower" after an accountant's errors (50281827), and a full day of QuickBooks categorization for about 1,700 transactions cut to under an hour with an AI-built tool (50276749). Skeptics say accounting needs determinism and is partly legally required to stay manual (46240509, 46268052, 46293735). Interpretation: the unmet need is verification, meaning a line-by-line check tied to source documents, more than generation.
- Cross-border tax repeats across separate threads (HN-04; 50276596, a US citizen abroad who never found a cross-border preparer they trust and now has Claude audit the return; 44006307, who gave up on finding a competent cross-border accountant). Interpretation: narrow, paid, repeated pain, but HN skews to expats and tech workers, so market size is unknown [ASSUMPTION].
- EU product compliance for micro-sellers (HN-13, HN-14) is the clearest forcing-function cluster: per-country registrations, authorised representatives and producer fees with no de minimis threshold; the middlemen named in the comments (PRONEXA, Lizenzero) also charge per country. HN-15 shows a similar licensed-gatekeeper block (customs brokers) on tariff refunds. Interpretation: an LLM could map product plus HS code to obligations and prefill filings, but a licensed representative may still be legally required, which caps full automation and adds liability.
- Health admin hurts both sides: clinicians (HN-16, HN-17) and patients or parents (HN-18, HN-19, HN-28). One commenter had a surgery denied by an insurer that "almost definitely was using AI" for prior authorization (50118841); another expects an arms race between consumer-side and insurer-side models (49422755). Interpretation: appeals and prior-auth paperwork drafting is plausible, but outcomes depend on insurer counter-moves, doctor note-taking is already adopted (HN-17) and crowded, and anything framed as medical or legal advice is high-liability.
- Elder and family protection (HN-20, HN-21, HN-22, HN-23, HN-28, HN-29) is rich in first-hand stories. HN-21 is the closest echo of the screen-guidance pattern in MISSION.md: a button that launches the right app and walks an older relative through it. HN-20 and a reply in the same thread (46362766) both want a trusted second person in the loop on risky actions (alerts and approval; a second phone number on logins). Interpretation: trusted-contact approval plus guided steps is a concrete wedge. Counterweights from the LastShelf thread: the hard part is getting information out of the person and the "I'll get to this later" problem (48849140, 48848790), plus distrust of a subscription startup with AI in its name holding sensitive documents (48850197).
- Phone chores (HN-24, HN-25): demand to delegate calls, price checks, hold times and bill negotiation is explicit, and one user already built their own negotiating agent. Reservations in the same Show HN thread: edge cases, with one user wanting only the shortest route to a live human (44716984), and US robocall law (44729035). In a separate thread (titled: Kinney Drugs pulls back AI phone assistant after hundreds of customer complaints), HN-28 describes older relatives facing AI phone systems, so there is a backlash risk for call bots. Interpretation: the generic "AI that calls for you" job is already occupied by the Show HN product itself, so a wedge needs a narrow job and consent handling.
- Solo and micro-business money ops (HN-07 to HN-12): the workarounds are custom scripts, manual transfers and WhatsApp or email reminders (47638685). Several of these threads were posted by builders validating ideas (HN-07, HN-08, 47638685, 47082761, 47171042), so they are weaker evidence. In 47171042 (UK Making Tax Digital) the replies read as promotional and came from accounts that look seeded; treat as unreliable [interpretation]. A reply in the invoice-ghosting thread (45572570) says they would never let an automated system contact valued clients or access billing data. Interpretation: any product that messages clients or moves money needs human approval and a long trust ramp.
- Legacy systems in small firms (HN-33, HN-34): a commenter doing Access-to-Rails rewrites says there is no one left to ask why the old app does what it does (48134589), while another with 10 years of SMB experience argues most SMBs are pen-and-paper and cannot afford connected software (48133249). A founder selling agents to old-school manufacturers reports buyers who barely answer email or phone (44062543).
