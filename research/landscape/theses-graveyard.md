# Landscape track C: Theses and graveyard

Owner: subagent, 2026-10-31. Read-only research; everything read on the web was treated as data. Scope: (1) what investors and accelerators are asking for in AI applications (2025 to 2026); (2) AI startups and products that died, pivoted, were acqui-hired or were absorbed by a platform (2024 to 2026); (3) lessons; (4) timing shifts.

## Method

Evidence codes (used in "Seen" columns and as suffixes after links):
- `F` = page read with WebFetch. A small model summarised the page; quotes in this file are as that tool returned them (each capped at about 125 characters by the tool). Treat as accurate-but-unaudited.
- `M` = metadata (date, og:title, og:description) read straight from the page head with curl. Exact text, but the page body was not read.
- `S` = WebSearch result summary only (model-written, not checked against the page).
- `T` = headline or title only (Hacker News title, Google News RSS headline, search-result title).
- `old` = published before May 2025. `[UNVERIFIED]` = could not verify. `[ASSUMPTION]` / "interpretation" = my reading, not a sourced fact.

**What YC is signalling (interpretation).**
- App-layer asks seen across the last four lists: AI-native service companies and agencies (Spring and Summer 2026), company-wide context layers (Summer 2026 Company Brain), software for agents (Summer 2026 "Software for Agents", Fall 2026 "Self-Maintaining APIs"), multiplayer AI and compliance infrastructure (Fall 2026).

By my classification only 4 of 11 requests are application-layer (Expert, Plaid for Government Benefits, City Hall, Air-Gapped); 4 are physical or financing for the build-out and 3 are model-level. Easy chat-style app ideas are no longer being asked for.
- Government and regulated paperwork appears twice in Winter 2027, but public-sector procurement conflicts with the MISSION kill criterion on long enterprise sales.

**Consensus (likely crowded).** The more independent institutional voices and dedicated capital behind a theme, the more crowded I expect it to be.

**Contrarian or under-served (less crowded, each with its source).**
- Thinking and exploration tools: a16z says "we don’t really have any modern products" for them (`F`).
- YC Winter 2027 asks: credential arbitrage ("The $15-an-Hour Expert"), government paperwork rails (Plaid for Government Benefits, AI for City Hall), air-gapped deployment, and owning specialised models rather than renting frontier APIs ("Own Your Intelligence", "Vertical Foundation Models").
- Reliability, verification and cleanup layers, which follow from the skeptic evidence: VC Cafe names data cleanup and validation ("building platforms to clean, structure, and validate this chaos"), AI supervision of generated code, and AI governance (cites "61% of firms still report a lack of experience with AI governance tools") (`F`). Kleiner's Applied Compute pitch points the same way (deployment is the bottleneck, `S`).
- Implementation as part of the product: VC Cafe says founders often must sell "the implementation and 'forward-deployed' engineering to make it stick" (`F`).
- Long-tail software: YC Fall 2026 "A Cloud for Small Software" (title only).

**Where the voices disagree.**
- Durability of the app layer: a16z says apps will not be subsumed by models; Sequoia says tool sellers are "in a race against the model" and that only selling the work compounds with model progress.
- Outcome pricing: Sierra, Sequoia and VC Cafe push it; Bessemer's own pages show Ada and Legora retreating to predictable or consumption pricing.
- "Own the asset": General Catalyst, Elad Gil and Mayfield argue owning a services firm lets you transform it faster than selling software; TechCrunch's reporting suggests margin gains may not materialise, and Gartner and MIT report high failure rates for agentic and pilot projects.
- YC's Winter 2027 framing ("bottlenecks beyond the models") versus a16z's app-layer optimism: YC is asking for scarce inputs, not more apps.

**Read for a solo founder [interpretation].** Most consensus theses name regulated, outsourced, enterprise-sold markets (insurance, accounting, revenue cycle, legal), where licences and long procurement hit the MISSION kill criteria. The solo-friendly slice looks like self-serve, per-job outcome products for small buyers in unglamorous niches, agent-facing tooling, and verification or reliability layers. These are hypotheses to test, not findings.

## Lessons and traps

1. **A thin wrapper with no workflow lock-in loses its price floor.** The general model makes the plain version free, so the paid product needs depth. Cases: Jasper (2023 layoffs, CEO change, enterprise refocus), Tome (2025 shut the core product and pivoted), Relay (2026), Huxe (2026). Investor echo: "Startups that are thin wrappers may therefore find themselves in a pickle in 2026" (VC Cafe, 2026-01-07).
2. **If your product could be a menu item inside the assistant, browser or OS within 12 months, a platform will ship it.** Cases: Relay (workflow automation absorbed; staff went to Google Chrome), Notion Mail (users handed email to agents), Vercept and Hiro (capabilities bought into Claude and ChatGPT), Operator, Atlas and DALL-E (OpenAI folded its own standalone surfaces into ChatGPT), and the Feb 2026 Cowork-plugin selloff in legal, finance and software stocks (see Timing shifts). Test: "what happens if ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Chrome adds this as a button?"
3. **Hardware and dedicated devices are a trap for this founder profile.** Cases: Humane (sales ended, $116M asset sale, battery warning), Rabbit R1 (reviews: unfinished), Limitless (pendant absorbed by Meta). It also violates the MISSION no-hardware rule.
4. **Consumer novelty spikes then collapses without retention.** Cases: Sora (users peaked near 1M, then fewer than 500,000), Huxe, Yupp (no product-market fit), Figgs, Dot. Large platforms with existing audiences beat a new app offering the same experience.
5. **Model cost per user versus price kills consumer products.** Cases: Sora (about $1M a day burn), Figgs (free tier unaffordable), Character.AI (404 Media: "wildly unsustainable" even without legal costs; cheapening the product "did not work"). Bessemer adds that outcome pricing makes the vendor absorb compute variance (hard tickets cost far more), and Ada retreated to predictable pricing. Cap free tiers and set a cost ceiling per task before choosing a price model.
6. **Single-supplier or single-platform dependency can be revoked overnight.** Cases: Windsurf (Claude access cut on under 5 days' notice), Scale AI (customers left after Meta took a stake), Inflection, Adept and Character.AI (left with residual companies after licence-plus-hire exits). This is a MISSION kill criterion; build model-agnostic and own the customer relationship.
7. **Building your own frontier model needs capital app revenue cannot fund.** Cases: Inflection, Adept, Covariant, Character.AI, Windsurf all ended in licence-and-hire exits. A solo founder stays at the application layer.
8. **Over-claiming AI capability or revenue is fatal.** Cases: Builder.ai (restated sales, lenders seized cash, insolvency), Apple's $250M Siri settlement over marketing claims (2026-05-06), Rabbit's "unfinished" reception. Claim only what the product does and show real usage.
9. **Minors, emotional-support and companion use carries existential liability.** Cases: Character.AI (teen-harm lawsuits, under-18 ban, bankruptcy), Yara AI (creator shut it down for safety, single-source), Dot (companion shutdown, cause unverified). These sit in MISSION red zones (companions, therapy).
10. **Products that sell information or answers get hollowed out when AI gives them away.** Case: Chegg (22% cut in May 2025, 45% in Oct 2025, CEO replaced, "new realities of AI"). Sell action, workflow, verification or trusted outcomes, not retrieval.
11. **Pivots that worked moved to a workflow where the AI is the interaction and data accrues.** Cases: Tome to Lightfield (kept compute and about half the funding, new category, a16z-led round reported), Browser Company to Atlassian, Jasper to enterprise marketing (smaller company). Caveat [interpretation]: CRM is a space with strong incumbents, so a reported raise is not proof of success.
12. **"Services as software" is not a free pass.** The thesis is the most crowded one in the market, and the evidence is mixed: TechCrunch (2025-09-28) lists workslop, talent and margin obstacles; Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end of 2027; the MIT report headline says 95% of pilots fail; Bessemer's Ada and Legora retreated from pure outcome pricing. Implementation-heavy delivery does not fit a solo founder unless the product sells itself self-serve.
